# NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 — Source Guide

> Joseph Technologies independent source guide. NIST remains the authoritative publisher. This guide does not establish compliance or claim NIST endorsement.

## Why this publication matters

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 provides a common, outcome-oriented language for managing cybersecurity risk. Version 2.0 added the Govern function and broadened the framework beyond critical infrastructure. It is useful for communicating desired outcomes across executives, risk owners, technical teams, suppliers, and assessors.

## Use it to

- establish cybersecurity governance outcomes and accountability;
- describe current and target cybersecurity posture with Organizational Profiles;
- communicate priorities without prescribing one implementation method;
- relate cybersecurity risk to enterprise risk management;
- organize measurements and improvement plans across Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover.

## Do not treat it as

- a mandatory control checklist;
- proof of regulatory compliance;
- a certification standard;
- a substitute for sector-specific legal or contractual requirements.

## Important source locations

- CSF 2.0 §2: the CSF Core, Organizational Profiles, and Tiers.
- CSF 2.0 §3: using the CSF.
- CSF 2.0 Appendix A: Core outcomes, including the Govern function.
- NIST SP 1299: CSF 2.0 overview and resource guide.
- NIST SP 1302: using CSF Tiers.
- NIST SP 1303: integrating cybersecurity risk with enterprise risk management.

## Governance artifacts this source can support

- cybersecurity governance framework;
- current and target Organizational Profiles;
- cybersecurity strategy and roadmap;
- risk appetite and priority statements;
- board and executive reporting model;
- supplier risk governance outcomes.
